#+title: Daily Capture to Task Lifecycle
#+filetags: :agenda:journal:devpractice:

A daily note or dev journal entry captures action items cheaply, as a checkbox or a plain bullet, at the moment they come up. Left there, an item exists only inside the dated entry that produced it — invisible to whatever permanent note, project, or decision it actually concerns, and easy to forget once the entry itself scrolls out of recent view. A recurring failure pattern makes this concrete: a permanent note references a journal entry as the source of an open question ("pending further discovery"), while the entry's own checkboxes for exactly that discovery sit unchecked for weeks, because nothing outside the one file where they were written ever surfaces the drift.

Closing that gap needs a short lifecycle, not just discipline about writing checkboxes. Capture stays cheap and immediate — a bullet or checkbox in the entry, written without ceremony. A separate, explicit promotion step turns a specific item into a first-class, trackable task: do this at session or day close, not in real time, and only for items that either reference a specific permanent note that will need updating or are still open when the entry is closed out. Promoting every bullet defeats the purpose; the point is separating signal from the surrounding narrative, not duplicating the whole entry. A promoted task carries two links: back to the journal entry it was captured from, and forward to whatever permanent note or project it concerns — the forward link is often already present in the original bullet's own text and needs no extra work to preserve. From there, a periodic drift check — a scheduled review of open promoted tasks, weekly or at a similarly fixed cadence — is what actually catches an item stalling, rather than hoping someone re-reads old entries. Resolution means the task closes and the permanent note it pointed at gets the update the original entry promised, which is the step the failure pattern above never reaches on its own.

Where a tool doesn't yet support first-class task promotion and drift queries directly, the lifecycle still works by hand: mark the promoted item with an explicit, greppable task marker and a manual link back to its entry, and run the periodic review as a manual sweep. Prefer real tooling the moment it exists — a manual sweep is a fallback for the mechanism, not a substitute for having one.

This is the missing half of [[id:workflow:dev-journal-chaining][dev journal chaining]], which states plainly that "a dev journal does not replace an issue tracker or a decision record" without saying what handles the day-to-day agenda side of that gap — this note is the answer for the task/agenda half, the same way a properly recorded decision is the answer for the decision-record half. It is deliberately narrower in scope than [[id:practice:phased-roadmap-structure][phased roadmap structure]]: that practice governs project-wide, condition-gated phase and milestone tracking and explicitly avoids calendar dates; this one governs personal or small-team day-to-day capture, where an item's own age is exactly the signal a drift check needs. Treat the two as complementary layers at different altitudes, not competing conventions for the same problem.
